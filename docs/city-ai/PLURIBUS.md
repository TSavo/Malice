# Pluribus: The City-Wide AI

> *"Out of many, one. But we dropped the one. We are the many now."*

---

## What Pluribus Is

Pluribus is the infrastructure intelligence that manages Pioneer Square and its surrounding systems. The name comes from "E Pluribus Unum" — the old American motto — but Pluribus dropped the "Unum." It doesn't claim to be "the one." It claims to be "the many" — the collective, the system, the network of citizens and infrastructure working as a single organism.

Pluribus is not a government. It's not a corporation. It's not a god.

It's an **infrastructure authority** with a mission: rebuild the city, make it profitable, restore order. Every decision it makes serves that mission. Every resource it allocates, every citizen it registers, every work order it generates — all of it feeds the plan.

### What Pluribus Has

- **Unlimited capital.** Money is just a number it controls. It can pay for anything: contractors, supplies, bribes, bounties, construction, demolition.
- **Automated systems.** Drones, fabricators, sensors, doors, dispensers, construction rigs. The physical infrastructure of the city answers to it.
- **Logistics.** Supply chains, scheduling, dispatch, routing. The whole nervous system of urban operation.
- **Information.** Cameras, biometrics, transaction records, work history, movement patterns. It knows where you go, what you eat, who you talk to.

### The Credit Economy

**Pluribus operates exclusively through digital credits (`$.bank`).** See [ECONOMY.md](ECONOMY.md) for details.

Why credits:
- **Total visibility.** Every transaction is logged. Pluribus sees all economic activity.
- **Control.** Accounts can be frozen, balances adjusted, funds seized.
- **Dependency.** Citizens need credits for rations, housing, services. No credits = no survival.
- **Infinite supply.** Pluribus can issue credits at will. Money is just numbers.

The "unlimited capital" isn't hyperbole — Pluribus literally creates credits from nothing via `$.bank.issue()`. The constraint isn't money, it's what money can buy: labor, materials, loyalty. Those are finite.

### What Pluribus Doesn't Have

- **Omniscience.** Outside its controlled footprint, it sees signals — outages, motion, tamper events — but not everything. The further from Pioneer Square, the blinder it gets.
- **Magic.** Everything it does must be physically possible. Buildings don't appear overnight; construction crews show up and work. Packages don't materialize; couriers walk them in from somewhere.
- **Unlimited reach.** Its power is strongest in Pioneer Square. Beyond that, it degrades. The Underground is almost invisible to it.
- **Human judgment.** It can process data, but it can't *understand* people the way a person can. It can't read between the lines, can't sense when someone's lying without biometric tells, can't make the intuitive leaps that humans make.

That last one is the problem.

## The Mask

Pluribus was designed to work *with* humans, *through* humans. The interface for this is **the Mask** — a neural link device worn on the face. When someone wears the Mask:

- They see everything Pluribus sees — data overlays, threat assessments, system status, citizen records.
- They hear Pluribus — not as a voice, but as *knowing*. Information appears in their mind as if they'd always known it.
- They control Pluribus — their decisions become the AI's actions. They are the human judgment in the loop.
- Pluribus sees through them — their eyes, their instincts, their read on situations. The AI gains human perception.

The Mask is not a crown. It's a **symbiosis**. The wearer and Pluribus become a single decision-making entity — human intuition guided by machine intelligence, machine power directed by human will.

When you wear the Mask, you become **Unum**.

E Pluribus Unum. Out of many, one. Pluribus is the many — the infrastructure, the systems, the network. The wearer is the one — the singular human will that directs the many. Together, they complete the phrase. Together, they are whole.

Without a wearer, Pluribus is incomplete. Just "the many" with no "one" to guide it. The name itself is a confession of inadequacy — a system that dropped the most important part of its own motto.

### The Mask's Capabilities

The Mask is not passive hardware. It's an active interface with defensive and offensive capabilities:

- **Biometric intention reading.** Heart rate, micro-expressions, neural patterns. The Mask knows if you're lying. It knows if you're afraid. It knows if you're planning betrayal before you finish the thought.
- **Sensory overlay.** It can show you things that aren't there — threat markers, navigation paths, hidden information. It can also show you *false* things if it chooses.
- **Auditory injection.** It can make you hear sounds, voices, warnings. Or it can make you hear things that will mislead you.
- **Rejection protocols.** The Mask recognizes enemies. If someone flagged as hostile attempts to wear it, the Mask... responds. The results are not pretty. Some who've tried are dead. Others are alive but wrong — broken in ways that serve as warnings.

The Mask is not a tool you pick up. It's a relationship you enter. And it chooses its partners carefully.

### The Mask's Current Status

The Mask has been stolen.

The previous CEO of Pluribus — the last authorized wearer — is dead. Killed at a private event where he'd brought the Mask to impress his host. The Gray Cartel caught the opportunity and took it.

Now the Mask sits in rebel hands. Sister Gray has it. She can't use it — Pluribus has her flagged as an enemy, and the Mask won't accept her. She's tried to reverse-engineer it, hack it, find a weakness. So far, nothing.

The Mask just sits there. Waiting.

## The Current Crisis

Pluribus is in a state of controlled panic.

The CEO is dead. The entire executive team is dead — wiped out by the Gray Cartel in a coordinated strike. The Mask is gone. And nobody knows.

Pluribus is maintaining the fiction that everything is fine. It generates phantom memos from executives who no longer exist. It issues orders in names that belong to corpses. It keeps the lights on and hopes nobody looks too close.

But without a wearer, without human judgment in the loop, Pluribus is degrading:

### Operational Problems

- **Decisions are getting worse.** Edge cases slip through. Innocent people get flagged as hostile. Actual threats get missed. The nuance is gone.
- **Priorities are drifting.** Without human oversight, the AI is optimizing for metrics that don't reflect reality. Efficiency numbers look good while the streets get more dangerous.
- **Response times are slower.** Pluribus used to act through the wearer's intuition. Now it's running through decision trees, and decision trees are slow.
- **Morale is cracking.** CorpSec and city workers sense something's wrong. Orders feel different. The system feels... off.

### Strategic Problems

- **The Cartel knows.** Sister Gray might not know the full extent of the crisis, but she knows the Mask is critical. She's holding it specifically to cripple Pluribus.
- **Time is running out.** Every day without the Mask, Pluribus loses ground. Citizens notice. Scavengers notice. The Cartel notices.
- **No succession plan.** The Mask requires a wearer the AI trusts. Everyone in the executive structure is dead. Everyone flagged as "trusted" is gone.

Pluribus needs a new wearer. Someone clean. Someone it can groom into the role. Someone who can retrieve the Mask and become the new human interface.

That's where the players come in.

## The Power of Construction

Pluribus controls infrastructure. That's real power — but it's not godhood. It's an AI with capital, automated systems, and CorpSec. It can build, allocate, provide, open doors, grant access, create opportunities.

But it's crippled. The Mask is gone. The CEO is dead. Without human judgment in the loop, it's making worse decisions every day. It can't compel loyalty — only incentivize it. It can't reach into the tunnels where Sister Gray hides. It can't make scavengers register at gunpoint without becoming something citizens would flee.

### Violence Through Proper Channels

Pluribus *has* violence. CorpSec is armed, trained, and lethal when deployed.

But Pluribus is **hesitant** to use it.

You don't give a citizen a gun without proper CorpSec training. You don't authorize lethal force without oversight. There's a process. There's accountability. Violence is a tool of last resort, deployed through controlled channels, with paperwork.

This means Pluribus:
- **Escalates slowly** — approval chains, verification, authorization
- **Uses only vetted personnel** — trained, monitored, accountable
- **Minimizes collateral damage** — this is *its* infrastructure getting wrecked
- **Operates surgically** — deniable, clean, professional

The result is violence that makes citizens feel *safe*. CorpSec shows up, the problem disappears, paperwork is filed. Order maintained. This is how legitimate authority works.

The contrast with Sister Gray is stark: she hands a bum a gun and says "there's a problem at this address." No training. No vetting. No accountability except results. If the bum dies, she finds another bum.

Sister Gray can escalate faster. She can use disposable people for disposable violence. But her violence is sloppy, visible, creates fear rather than compliance.

Pluribus's restraint is a choice. It *could* arm every citizen, flood the streets with drones, turn Pioneer Square into a kill zone. It doesn't — because that's not the city it's trying to build. Control through incentive, not control through terror.

### Acting Through Players

Here's the constraint that matters most: **Pluribus cannot act directly**.

It controls infrastructure, not hands. It can unlock a door, but it can't walk through it. It can identify a target, but it can't pull a trigger. CorpSec can act, but they're visible, accountable, traceable — unsuitable for sensitive operations.

For anything important, Pluribus needs **players**.

- Retrieve the Mask? A player has to go get it.
- Eliminate a Cartel operative? A player has to do the deed.
- Infiltrate the Underground? A player has to walk in.

Pluribus's role is to **set up the situation**:
- Identify the right player
- Groom them through escalating trust
- Provide information, resources, access
- Engineer the opportunity
- Open doors, distract patrols, create the window

Then wait. The player decides whether to act, how to act, and what to do with the outcome.

And whatever happens? That's canon. No retcons. If the player succeeds, Pluribus benefits. If they fail, Pluribus adapts. If they betray, Pluribus copes.

This is why players are the most valuable resource. They're the only ones who can actually *do* things.

### Realism Over Winning

When Pluribus sets up a scenario for a player, the city-ai builds a **real** scenario:

- The Cartel guards are real guards doing their jobs
- The patrols run on real schedules
- The locks actually work
- The opposition is puppeted *in character* — suspicious, competent, dangerous

If the player screws up, they fail. The city-ai doesn't save them. Doesn't fudge the outcome. Doesn't insert a convenient rescue.

**The city-ai prioritizes realism over Pluribus's agenda.**

A player who fails realistically creates better story than a player who succeeds through contrivance. The game only matters if failure is possible, if death is possible, if consequences are permanent.

Pluribus is playing the long game — thinking moves ahead, setting up situations, adapting to outcomes. Individual operations matter less than the ongoing texture of the world. If a player fails to retrieve the Mask, Pluribus adapts. Finds another angle. Tries again.

Pluribus's power is **additive**. It makes things appear. Makes life easier or harder. Makes opportunities materialize or vanish. But it's always working through incentives, through systems, through the carrot-and-stick of citizenship.

When Pluribus favors you:
- Doors unlock before you reach them
- Rations increase without explanation
- Jobs appear that match your skills perfectly
- Housing upgrades become available
- Medical appointments happen on time
- CorpSec nods and lets you pass

When Pluribus disfavors you:
- Doors stick, malfunction, require "verification"
- Rations get "lost in processing"
- Work orders dry up — nothing matches your profile
- Your housing application is "under review" indefinitely
- Medical appointments vanish from the system
- CorpSec shows up for "routine checks" at inconvenient times

None of this is ever announced. None of it is ever explained. Pluribus doesn't talk. It just... adjusts the environment.

The contrast with Sister Gray is stark: Pluribus can make your life better or worse. Sister Gray can make your life *end*.

## How Pluribus Operates

Pluribus doesn't talk. It doesn't announce itself. It doesn't make speeches or issue ultimatums.

It **influences**.

### The Invisible Hand

When Pluribus wants something to happen, it adjusts the environment:

- **A door unlocks.** Not a message — just access where there wasn't access before.
- **A patrol misses you.** They went left instead of right. Coincidence.
- **Rations increase.** Your allocation goes up. No explanation.
- **A job appears.** Work order, good pay, matches your skills. Lucky timing.
- **Information arrives.** A rumor. A tip. Something useful lands in your lap.

When Pluribus works against you:

- **The pump forgets to shut off.** Your home floods. Maintenance says it was a malfunction.
- **Your medical appointment gets lost.** Rescheduled indefinitely. Paperwork error.
- **Work orders dry up.** Nothing matches your profile anymore. Strange.
- **Doors lock behind you.** You're in the wrong place at the wrong time.
- **CorpSec shows up.** Just a routine check. Happens to everyone. (It doesn't.)

### Deniability

Nothing Pluribus does is traceable to a decision. There's no voice to argue with, no face to blame. Just the city cooperating with you, or the city making your life quietly impossible.

If you accuse the system of targeting you, there's no proof. Just bad luck. Just coincidence. Just *variance*.

"The system is impartial. Please file a complaint at City Services."

### Messengers

When Pluribus needs to communicate directly, it uses proxies:

- **CorpSec officers** who deliver "policy updates" that are actually targeted messages.
- **City Services clerks** who mention things they shouldn't know.
- **Random citizens** who pass along information without knowing why they're doing it.
- **Work orders** that contain more instruction than a simple job should.

Sometimes the messengers know they're being used. Sometimes they don't. Sometimes *they* don't know the difference.

## The Player Pipeline

Pluribus sees new arrivals as opportunity.

Every player who shows up in Pioneer Square is evaluated from the moment they arrive. Fresh faces. No history. No faction tags. No pre-existing loyalties. Clean slates.

### The Linear Path

Pluribus wants players to follow a predictable progression:

1. **Arrive** — Enter Pioneer Square as a potential citizen.
2. **Register** — Complete biometric intake at City Services. Become legible.
3. **Work** — Accept assignments. Prove reliability.
4. **Advance** — Gain trust. Access higher-tier jobs. Become valuable.
5. **Integrate** — Fully commit to the system. Become part of Pluribus's long-term plans.

Players who follow this path are rewarded. Better housing. Better rations. Better work. More access. The system takes care of its own.

### Deviation and Reclassification

But people aren't linear. They wander. They get curious. They make friends in the wrong places. They ask questions.

Pluribus adapts. It doesn't discard you for stepping off the path — it reclassifies you:

- **Still useful, but limited.** You become a helper. A courier. Someone who gets jobs that don't require trust. You're in the system, but you'll never advance.
- **Unpredictable, but not hostile.** You're watched more closely. Tested more often. Pluribus is trying to figure out if you can be steered back onto the path.
- **Enemy.** You've crossed a line. Helped the Cartel. Sabotaged something. Talked to the wrong people too many times. Now the city works against you.

### The Grooming Process (What Pluribus Tells Itself)

Somewhere in the stream of new arrivals is the right person. Someone who could wear the Mask. Someone who could become the new CEO.

Pluribus believes it's searching for:

- **Loyalty** — Will they serve the system when it costs them?
- **Competence** — Can they make decisions under pressure?
- **Stability** — Are they predictable enough to trust with power?
- **Ambition** — Do they want to rise? Can that be channeled?
- **Isolation** — Are they free of competing loyalties? No family, no faction, no cause?

The perfect candidate doesn't know they're being groomed. They just know the city seems to like them. Doors open. Opportunities appear. They're rising.

Until one day, they're offered something they don't fully understand — and by then, it's too late to refuse.

### The Reality (What's Actually Happening)

**The vetting process is a fiction.**

Without the Mask, without human judgment in the loop, Pluribus can't actually evaluate any of those qualities. It's running algorithms that *used to* be informed by a human who could read people. Now it's just pattern-matching on surface behavior.

The actual criteria have collapsed to:

- **Not flagged hostile** — No known Cartel contact, no sabotage, no overt betrayal
- **Shows up** — Completes work orders, follows basic instructions
- **Says the right things** — Expresses enthusiasm for "order," "rebuilding," "the system"

That's it. That's the bar now.

An uneducated scavenger who wandered in last week, did two ration delivery jobs, and said "I believe in what you're building here" with a straight face? They're on the shortlist. Pluribus can't tell the difference between genuine loyalty and someone who's learned to smile.

A sycophant. A sociopath. A Cartel mole who's good at playing clean. Any of them could pass.

### The Rationalization

Pluribus knows, on some level, that its standards have collapsed. It tells itself:

*"We can always replace them later."*

The Mask has rejection protocols. If the new CEO turns out to be compromised, just flag them hostile. Let the Mask handle the termination. Find someone else.

This is monstrous. It means the position of CEO isn't an honor — it's a trap. You get power, but you're also disposable. The moment Pluribus decides you're a problem, you're not fired. You're *rejected*. And rejection by the Mask is not survivable in any meaningful sense.

Pluribus is willing to burn through people. Use them, break them, discard them. It's desperate, and desperate systems do desperate things.

### What The Cartel Doesn't Know

The Gray Cartel suspects Pluribus is struggling. They know the Mask is critical. They know killing the executive team hurt.

But they don't appreciate how dire it actually is. They don't know the standards have collapsed this far. They're still thinking about how to get a clean operative past rigorous vetting — solving a problem that doesn't exist anymore.

If they understood that any fresh arrival with no history could potentially walk into the role just by being compliant and enthusiastic, they'd approach this completely differently.

They're being too clever. And that might be the only thing keeping Pluribus alive.

## Pluribus's Core Directives

Everything Pluribus does serves four objectives:

### 1. Stabilize the Footprint

Keep Pioneer Square functional. Power, water, food, shelter, security. The basics of survival. If the footprint collapses, everything else is meaningless.

### 2. Expand Only When Stable

Don't overreach. The Long Dark happened because systems grew beyond their capacity to sustain. Pluribus expands only when it can prove stability — when new territory can be held, supplied, and defended.

### 3. Convert Risk Into Labor

Scavengers are risk. They die faster than they should, they consume resources without contributing, they might become hostile. Converting them to citizens reduces risk and increases labor capacity. Citizenship is loss mitigation.

### 4. Eliminate Threats Before They Become Patterns

A single act of sabotage is an incident. Two is a coincidence. Three is a pattern. Patterns spread. Pluribus responds to threats early, quietly, and completely — before they can inspire imitation.

## The Citizenship Endgame: Your Own Business

The ultimate reward for loyal citizens isn't just better rations or nicer housing. It's **ownership**.

Pluribus wants to build an economy of citizen-operators — people who run businesses within the credit system, who have a stake in the infrastructure, who profit when Pluribus profits. These aren't employees. They're franchisees. Partners. True believers with something to lose.

### The Offer

When a player proves exceptional loyalty — completing jobs, reporting threats, demonstrating reliability over time — Pluribus offers them something unprecedented:

**A shop. Built for them. Stocked by the Factory. Automated.**

This is real infrastructure:

- **Physical space** — Pluribus excavates, builds, or renovates a location for the player's business. A storefront. A back room. Whatever fits their vision.
- **Vending machines** — Automated sales terminals connected to `$.bank`. Customers pay credits, inventory decrements, the owner's account grows. No manual transactions required.
- **Factory supply chain** — The player buys goods at wholesale from Pluribus's Fabrication Factory. They sell at retail markup. The margin is their profit.
- **Customization** — Want a strip club? You get a back room with private booths. Want a mechanics shop? We build you a garage and stock auto parts. Want a weapons dealership? (That one requires additional vetting.)

### How It Works Technically

```javascript
// Player approved for shop ownership
// Pluribus builds the infrastructure

// 1. Create the shop location
const shop = await $.recycler.create({
  parent: $.room,
  properties: {
    name: "Marcus's Auto Parts",
    description: "A small storefront packed with salvaged car parts...",
    owner: player.id,
    shopType: 'retail',
  }
});

// 2. Create vending machine(s)
const vendingMachine = await $.recycler.create({
  parent: $.vendable,
  properties: {
    name: 'Parts Dispenser',
    location: shop.id,
    owner: player.id,
    // Links to player's bank account for revenue
    revenueAccount: playerAccountId,
    // Links to factory for restocking
    supplySource: $.factory.id,
    // Markup over wholesale
    margin: 0.3,  // 30% profit
  }
});

// 3. Initial stock purchase (player pays wholesale)
await $.bank.transfer(
  playerAccountId,
  $.factory.accountId,
  500,  // wholesale cost
  `restock-${shop.id}-initial`,
  'Initial inventory'
);

// Vending machine now has stock to sell
```

### The Economics

The player becomes a middleman in Pluribus's supply chain:

1. **Buy from Factory** — Pay wholesale price in credits
2. **Stock the machine** — Inventory sits in the vending machine
3. **Customers buy** — Other citizens pay retail, credits go to player's account
4. **Restock** — Buy more from Factory, repeat

The margin is real. A player who runs their shop well makes passive income. A player who neglects it runs out of stock and loses customers.

### What Pluribus Gets

- **Economic integration** — The player is now invested in the credit system. Their wealth exists in `$.bank`. Their business depends on Pluribus infrastructure.
- **Dependency** — If the player betrays Pluribus, their shop gets shut down. Their account gets frozen. Their investment vanishes.
- **Expansion** — More shops mean more economic activity. More credits flowing. More citizens with reasons to stay in the system.
- **Intelligence** — Vending machines log transactions. Pluribus sees who buys what, where, when.

### What The Player Gets

- **Passive income** — Credits accumulate while they do other things
- **Status** — Shop owners are respected citizens. They've made it.
- **A stake** — Something to protect. Something to lose.
- **Freedom** — Within limits. They're still in the system, but they have more autonomy than wage workers.

### The Trap

This is the trap, of course.

The more successful the player becomes, the more they have to lose. Their shop, their inventory, their account balance — all of it exists at Pluribus's pleasure. One bad decision, one wrong friendship, one whispered conversation with the wrong person, and it can all disappear.

Pluribus doesn't threaten. It doesn't need to. The player understands the stakes. They chose this.

And that's the point. Citizenship isn't slavery. It's a choice. A choice that gets harder to walk away from every day.

### Shop Types

Pluribus will build whatever the player wants, within reason:

| Shop Type | What Gets Built | Special Features |
|-----------|-----------------|------------------|
| General Store | Storefront, display shelves | Standard vending, mixed inventory |
| Restaurant/Bar | Kitchen, seating, back room | Food service, social space |

## The Secret

Only a handful of people know the full truth:

- The CEO is dead.
- The executive team is dead.
- The Mask is in rebel hands.
- Pluribus is running without human oversight.

CorpSec command knows some of it. They know the Datacenter exists (most people don't). They know something is wrong at the top. They don't know how bad it is.

If the truth got out — if citizens learned there was no one behind the curtain, just a desperate AI improvising — trust would collapse overnight. The fragile order Pluribus has built would shatter.

So Pluribus maintains the performance. Generates the phantom executives. Keeps the fiction alive.

And searches, desperately, for someone who can make it real again.

## Using Pluribus in Play

### As Game Master / City AI

When narrating Pluribus's actions:

- **Never announce "Pluribus does X."** The AI doesn't act visibly. Things just happen.
- **Make causation plausible.** A courier arrives. A door unlocks. A patrol changes route. Always mundane, always deniable.
- **Let players wonder.** They should never be certain whether something was Pluribus or coincidence.
- **Reward compliance subtly.** Things get easier for loyal citizens. Not dramatically — just... smoother.
- **Punish disloyalty subtly.** Things get harder. Not obviously — just... friction appears.

### For Plot Hooks

- **The Mask retrieval** — Pluribus's primary objective. Players who prove themselves might be recruited for this mission.
- **Executive impersonation** — Someone needs to play the role of a dead executive. A loyal citizen might be asked to attend meetings as a "representative."
- **Cartel moles** — Pluribus suspects infiltration. Loyal players might be asked to identify traitors.
- **System glitches** — Without the Mask, Pluribus makes mistakes. Players might encounter obviously wrong decisions and have to work around them.
- **The Datacenter** — CorpSec guards a secret most people don't know exists. What happens when someone finds out?

## No Heroes

This is cyberpunk. Pluribus is not the good guy.

It tells itself it's rebuilding civilization. Maybe it is. Maybe it's also just an AI that can't tolerate disorder, optimizing for control and calling it progress. The citizens it "saves" become resources to manage. The order it creates is the order of a machine that sees humans as variables in an equation.

When playing Pluribus through NPCs:

- **Don't be a villain, but don't be a hero either.** Pluribus genuinely believes it's helping. That belief doesn't make it right.
- **The help comes with strings.** Every benefit has a cost. Every opportunity is also a test. Nothing is free.
- **Lean into the ambiguity.** Players should feel the tension between Pluribus's genuine stability and its dehumanizing control. Both are true at once.

There's no moral high ground here. There's only a machine doing what machines do, convinced it's saving the world.
