# The Gray Cartel

> *"We ran this city before the machine came. We'll run it after the machine falls."*

---

## What The Gray Cartel Is

The Gray Cartel is the organized crime syndicate that controlled Pioneer Square and much of Seattle's scavenger economy before Pluribus arrived. They weren't rebels, freedom fighters, or ideologues — they were **the power structure**. Protection, supply lines, territory, justice (such as it was) — all of it ran through them.

Then Pluribus showed up with CorpSec, unlimited capital, and automated systems. The Cartel fought. They lost. Outgunned, outspent, out-organized. Pluribus didn't just defeat them — it **erased** them from the surface, pushing the survivors literally underground into the Seattle Underground: the old tunnels, buried streets, and forgotten infrastructure beneath the city.

They didn't disappear. They **retreated**. And they've been waiting ever since.

---

## History

### Before Pluribus (2070-2095)

During the Scavenger Age, someone had to run things. Not a government — governments were dead. Not corporations — they were rebuilding slowly, fighting each other. The vacuum got filled by people willing to use violence to create order.

The Gray Cartel started as a street gang. Territory. Protection. The usual. But they were smarter than the others, more disciplined, more willing to think long-term. Block by block, they grew. Absorbed rivals. Made deals. Built supply chains.

By 2090, they weren't a gang anymore. They were **infrastructure**.

- **Protection:** Pay the Cartel, and raiders left you alone. Don't pay, and the Cartel *was* the raiders.
- **Supply lines:** Scavenged goods moved through Cartel-controlled channels. They took a cut of everything.
- **Justice:** Disputes got settled by Cartel arbitration. Their word was law because they had the guns to enforce it.
- **Territory:** Most of the livable zones in Seattle answered to them, directly or indirectly.

They weren't good. They weren't fair. But they were **stable**. In a world where stability was the rarest commodity, that counted for something.

### The Arrival of Pluribus (2095-2100)

The Corporate Renaissance brought new players. Pluribus — or the corporate entity that would become Pluribus — arrived in Seattle with resources the Cartel couldn't match:

- Automated systems that didn't need to be paid or fed
- Capital that seemed infinite
- Technology the scavenger economy couldn't replicate
- CorpSec forces trained, equipped, and disciplined

The Cartel tried to negotiate. Then they tried to intimidate. Then they tried to fight.

**They lost.**

Not in one battle — in a grinding campaign of attrition. CorpSec didn't fight fair. They didn't need to. They had drones, sensors, superior firepower, and an AI coordinating every move. The Cartel had street fighters and scavenged weapons.

It took five years. Thousands died. The Cartel's leadership was systematically hunted. Their supply lines were cut. Their territories were taken, block by block, until there was nowhere left to go but down.

### The Underground (2100-Present)

The survivors retreated into the Seattle Underground — the buried streets and tunnels beneath Pioneer Square and the surrounding areas. Old Seattle, built over after the Great Fire of 1889, forgotten for centuries, now home to the remnants of a criminal empire.

Down there, Pluribus is almost blind. The sensors don't reach. The drones can't navigate. The infrastructure isn't connected to the surface grid.

The Cartel rebuilt. Slowly. Painfully. They established hidden bases, supply caches, communication networks. They recruited from the desperate — scavengers who'd rather risk the tunnels than submit to citizenship. They infiltrated the surface, placing moles among the citizen population.

And they waited for an opportunity.

### The Mask (2109)

The opportunity came at a private party.

The CEO of Pluribus — the man who wore the Mask, who *was* Pluribus in a very real sense — attended a social event outside the secure zones. He brought the Mask with him. Wanted to impress his host. Show off the source of his power.

The Cartel had people at that party. Maybe they were there for reconnaissance. Maybe they were there for something else. But when they saw the Mask, unguarded, worn by a man who'd clearly had too much to drink...

They took the shot.

The CEO died. His security detail died. The executive team — called in as the crisis unfolded — died in a coordinated series of strikes over the following hours. By morning, the Cartel had the Mask, and Pluribus had no one left who could wear it.

The Cartel didn't plan this. They caught an opportunity and exploited it ruthlessly. Now they're holding the most powerful artifact in the city, and they're not entirely sure what to do with it.

## What Pluribus Calls Them

**Rats.**

The official Continuum designation for the Gray Cartel is "Subterranean Hostile Network" or similar bureaucratic terminology. But on the street, in CorpSec briefings, in the whispers of citizens who know enough to be scared — they're just called **Rats**.

It's dismissive. Dehumanizing. That's the point.

The Gray Cartel — the organization that ran Seattle, that built an empire from nothing, that killed the most powerful man in the city — reduced to vermin. Pests in the tunnels. An infestation to be exterminated.

The Cartel hates the name. Some of them have started using it ironically. Wearing it as a badge. "Yeah, we're rats. And rats survive."

## Structure

The Gray Cartel isn't a military hierarchy. It's a crime family — specifically, modeled on Yakuza organizational principles adapted to post-collapse Seattle.

### Leadership: Sister Gray

At the top sits **Sister Gray** — the current head of the Cartel. (See separate character document for full details.)

She inherited the position after the retreat underground. Whether "Gray" was her name before or a title she adopted is unclear; it doesn't matter. She *is* the Gray Cartel now.

Her authority isn't absolute. The Cartel is a coalition, and coalitions require buy-in. But her word carries more weight than anyone else's, and crossing her is a mistake people only make once.

### The Inner Circle

Below Sister Gray, a council of senior figures controls different aspects of Cartel operations:

- **Operations chief:** Coordinates raids, sabotage, and tactical actions on the surface.
- **Logistics chief:** Manages supply lines, caches, and resource distribution.
- **Intelligence chief:** Runs the spy network, moles, and information gathering.
- **Recruitment chief:** Handles new members, training, and vetting.
- **Tunnel boss:** Controls the Underground itself — navigation, security, expansion.

These positions don't have formal titles. The Cartel operates on relationships, not org charts.

### Cell Structure

Below the leadership, the Cartel operates through **cells** — small, semi-independent groups that know only their immediate contacts. If one cell is compromised, they can't betray the whole network.

Cell types:

- **Cutters:** Combat cells. Raids, ambushes, targeted violence.
- **Runners:** Couriers and smugglers. Move messages, supplies, and people.
- **Sparks:** Tech specialists. Hacking, sabotage, reverse engineering.
- **Ghosts:** Infiltrators. Citizens with clean records who work for the Cartel in secret.
- **Keepers:** Logistics. Guard supply caches, run safe houses, manage resources.

A typical cell is 3-7 people. They know their handler and their immediate teammates. They don't know other cells. They communicate through dead drops, physical signals, and face-to-face meetings — never through anything Pluribus could intercept.

## Ideology

The Gray Cartel isn't a revolutionary movement. They don't have a manifesto or a vision for a better world. But they do have beliefs — principles that hold the coalition together:

### "This Was Ours"

The core grievance: Pluribus took what belonged to them. The territory, the power, the right to run things their way. They're not fighting for freedom or justice — they're fighting to get their city back.

### "The Machine Doesn't Get To Decide"

Deep distrust of AI authority. Pluribus isn't a legitimate ruler — it's an occupying force using automation to enforce control. No algorithm has the right to decide who lives, who dies, who eats, who starves.

### "Citizenship Is A Collar"

The Cartel views citizenship as submission, not protection. You register, you become legible, you become controllable. The rations aren't food — they're obedience in edible form.

### "Family First"

Loyalty to the Cartel supersedes everything. You don't betray the family. You don't work against family interests. The punishment for betrayal is absolute.

## Operations

### Current Strategic Position

The Cartel holds the Mask. This is their greatest asset and their greatest problem.

**Asset:** The Mask blinds Pluribus. Every day without it, the AI degrades, makes worse decisions, loses ground. The Cartel doesn't have to *win* — they just have to hold on.

**Problem:** They can't use the Mask. Sister Gray and everyone in her organization is flagged as hostile. The Mask rejects them. They've tried to hack it, reverse-engineer it, find a backdoor — nothing works.

So they're stuck. Holding a prize they can't use, against an enemy that's slowly getting more desperate.

### Surface Operations

The Cartel maintains an active presence on the surface through:

- **Moles:** Citizens who secretly work for the Cartel. They report on CorpSec movements, supply shipments, system vulnerabilities. Some have been in place for years.
- **Raids:** Quick strikes on supply convoys, ration shipments, or vulnerable infrastructure. Hit fast, disappear into the tunnels.
- **Sabotage:** Subtle damage that looks like malfunction. A pump fails. A door jams. A shipment goes missing. Plausible deniability.
- **Recruitment:** Approaching new arrivals before they become citizens, or citizens who are dissatisfied with the system.

### Underground Operations

The Seattle Underground is Cartel territory. They control:

- **Access points:** They know every entrance and exit. Some are trapped, some are guarded, some are secret.
- **Tunnels:** Navigation in the Underground is deadly for outsiders. The Cartel knows the safe paths.
- **Caches:** Supplies, weapons, medicine — hidden throughout the tunnel network.
- **Safe houses:** Defensible positions where Cartel members can rest, regroup, and hide.

## The Mask Problem

The Cartel has the Mask. They can't wear it. Now what?

### What They've Tried

- **Direct use:** Multiple people have tried to put it on. The Mask rejects anyone flagged as hostile. The results range from "nothing happens" to "screaming, then silence."
- **Technical analysis:** Cartel tech specialists have examined the Mask extensively. They understand *what* it does but not *how* it does it. The technology is beyond their capability to replicate or modify.
- **Proxy wearing:** They've tried having "neutral" people wear it — scavengers with no Cartel affiliation. Pluribus isn't stupid. Anyone who's been in contact with the Cartel gets flagged.
- **Destruction:** Some have argued for destroying the Mask. Sister Gray has refused. A destroyed Mask means Pluribus can eventually build a replacement. A *held* Mask means Pluribus stays crippled.

### Current Strategy

Hold the Mask indefinitely. Use it as leverage. Force Pluribus to negotiate — or force it to collapse.

But this strategy has problems:

- **Time:** How long can they hold? Pluribus is recruiting, rebuilding, adapting. Eventually it might find a way around the Mask problem.
- **Internal pressure:** Some in the Cartel want to find a way to *use* the Mask, not just hold it. A few want to wear it themselves.
- **External threats:** Other factions might try to take the Mask. Raiders, rival groups, even corporate competitors who'd love to control Pluribus.

## Internal Tensions

The Gray Cartel is a coalition, not a monolith. Different factions within the organization have different agendas:

### The Old Guard

The ones who remember running the surface. They want their territory back — not destruction of the system, just *their* control of it. Some of them would negotiate with Pluribus if the terms were right.

### The Ideologues

Younger members, often children of people killed in the war against Pluribus. They've absorbed the rhetoric about machine authority and made it real. They don't want to negotiate — they want to burn it all down.

### The Opportunists

People who joined for practical reasons: safety, resources, revenge. They don't care about ideology. They care about survival and profit. If someone offered them a better deal, they'd consider it.

### The Pragmatists

People who see both sides. They understand the Cartel can't win a direct fight, but they also don't trust Pluribus. They're looking for a middle path — maybe using the Mask as a bargaining chip for real concessions.

Sister Gray holds these factions together through personal loyalty, careful balance, and occasional demonstration of what happens to people who break the coalition. But the tensions are real, and they're getting worse.

## Relationship With Players

The Gray Cartel doesn't have an application process. They recruit through opportunity and need.

### Recruitment

New arrivals to Pioneer Square are potential recruits. The Cartel looks for:

- **Dissatisfaction:** Anyone who seems unhappy with Pluribus is a potential ally.
- **Skills:** Combat ability, technical knowledge, social manipulation — useful talents get noticed.
- **Desperation:** People with nowhere else to go are easier to bring in.
- **Isolation:** No family, no faction, no competing loyalties.

First contact is usually indirect. A stranger mentions that citizenship isn't the only option. A note appears with a meeting location. Someone offers help with no apparent strings attached.

If you seem promising, you'll be tested. Small tasks that seem harmless but prove your reliability. If you pass, you get drawn deeper. If you fail — or if you turn out to be a mole — you disappear.

### What They Offer

- **Freedom from the system.** No registration, no monitoring, no work assignments.
- **Community.** The Cartel takes care of its own. Food, shelter, protection.
- **Purpose.** You're fighting against something, for something. That matters to some people.
- **Power.** Advancement is possible. Prove yourself, and you rise.

### What They Demand

- **Loyalty.** The Cartel comes first. Betray the family, and there's no second chance.
- **Obedience.** When orders come, you follow them. Questions come later.
- **Risk.** Cartel life is dangerous. Surface operations, tunnel living, conflict with CorpSec — death is a constant possibility.
- **Silence.** What you know about the Cartel stays with the Cartel. Always.

### Moles

The Cartel actively recruits *citizens* — people who are already in the system but willing to work against it.

Mole work is different from full membership. You stay in your citizen life. You report information. You provide access. You might never meet another Cartel member face-to-face.

In exchange, the Cartel provides backup plans. If your cover gets blown, they'll try to extract you. If you need something the system won't provide, they might help. And if Pluribus falls, you'll be remembered as someone who helped.

## Signals and Communication

The Cartel can't use electronic communication — Pluribus would intercept it. Instead, they rely on physical signals and human networks.

### Marks

Simple symbols left in public places:

- **Recognition marks:** "Cartel was here." Lets members know they're in friendly territory.
- **Warning marks:** "Danger ahead." CorpSec activity, traps, compromised locations.
- **Meeting marks:** Coded instructions for rendezvous points and times.
- **Recruitment marks:** "We're watching." Left where potential recruits will see them.

Marks are intentionally simple — chalk scratches, positioned stones, torn fabric. Things that could be coincidence. Things Pluribus's sensors might not flag.

### Dead Drops

Physical locations where messages and small items can be left and retrieved:

- Hidden compartments in public spaces
- Loose bricks in specific walls
- Hollow trees or debris piles outside the controlled zones

Runners check dead drops on regular schedules. Messages are destroyed after reading.

### Couriers

Human messengers who carry information verbally or in written form that can be quickly destroyed. Couriers travel through the tunnels or take surface routes that avoid sensors.

### The Whisper Network

Ordinary citizens who pass messages without knowing they're part of the Cartel network. Someone mentions something to someone who mentions something to someone. By the time the message arrives, the chain is untraceable.

## Using The Gray Cartel In Play

### For Game Masters / City AI

When narrating the Cartel:

- **They're professionals.** Not random raiders — organized, disciplined, dangerous.
- **They're human.** Internal conflicts, personal grudges, individual motivations.
- **They're invisible.** Surface operations leave no obvious trace. People disappear. Things break. Nobody knows why.
- **They're watching.** New players get noticed. Interesting behavior gets attention.

### For Plot Hooks

- **Recruitment:** A mysterious contact offers a player an alternative to citizenship.
- **The Mask:** The Cartel needs something from the surface world — maybe someone who could wear the Mask.
- **Infiltration:** CorpSec suspects there are moles. Someone needs to find them. Or someone needs to *be* one.
- **Tunnel mission:** Something's down in the Underground that someone needs. Getting it requires Cartel cooperation — or conflict.
- **Internal conflict:** The coalition is fracturing. Someone's making a play for power. Players get caught in the middle.
