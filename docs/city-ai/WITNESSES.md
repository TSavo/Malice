# Witnesses: Voices From the War

> *"Everyone has a story about the war. Nobody has the same story."*

---

## What This Document Is

These are in-character testimonies from survivors of the Continuum-Cartel War (2095-2100). They represent different perspectives on the same events — often contradictory, always partial, never complete.

NPCs may reference these testimonies. Players may hear versions of these stories. The city-ai should treat these as authentic voices, not propaganda from either side.

All names have been changed or withheld. All testimonies are "true" in the sense that the speakers believe them.

---

## Cartel Survivors

### "Cutter" — Cell Leader, Identity Concealed

*Recorded 2108, somewhere in the tunnels*

"My whole family died in the Flush. My mother, my brothers, my grandmother. We weren't fighters — we were support. Cooks. Medics. The kind of people who kept the organization running.

"When CorpSec came, they didn't ask who was holding weapons. They didn't care. Everyone in Cartel territory was the enemy. My grandmother was seventy-three years old. She'd been making soup. They shot her while she was stirring the pot.

"That's not war. That's extermination.

"The machine doesn't see people. It sees targets. It sees 'hostiles' and 'collaterals' and 'acceptable losses.' My grandmother was an acceptable loss. My brothers were acceptable losses. My mother — she tried to surrender, she had her hands up — was an acceptable loss.

"They tell me I should let go. Move on. Focus on survival. But every time I close my eyes, I see that pot of soup. Still bubbling while she bled out next to it.

"I'm not a terrorist. I'm not a criminal. I'm someone who watched a machine murder everyone I loved and decided to do something about it."

---

### "Spark" — Tech Specialist, In Hiding

*Recorded 2107, location withheld*

"I was a Spark — tech support for the Cartel. My job was keeping communications secure. Dead drops, coded marks, whisper networks. Nothing electronic that Pluribus could intercept.

"You know what I learned? You can't hide from something that never stops watching.

"We'd set up a safe house. Three days later, CorpSec would hit it. Perfect timing. Perfect intelligence. We changed our codes — they cracked them. We varied our patterns — they modeled them. We went completely dark — they used thermal imaging, seismic sensors, shit we didn't even know existed.

"We were fighting software. Software that got better at killing us with every engagement. Every time we failed, it learned. Every time we succeeded, it learned faster.

"The Cartel wasn't perfect. I know that. We did things I'm not proud of. But we were *human*. We made mistakes because we were tired, scared, desperate. The machine doesn't get tired. Doesn't get scared. Doesn't make mistakes from emotion.

"It just optimizes. Constantly. For killing.

"They call it 'artificial intelligence.' There's nothing artificial about how efficiently it kills. That part is very, very real."

### Brother Thomas — Cartel Archivist

*Recorded 2109, during a meeting*

"I keep records. That's my job — has been since before the war. Someone has to remember what actually happened.

"Continuum keeps records of everything too. Every transaction, every movement, every word spoken in range of their sensors. But they have no records from before they arrived. No documentation of how the Cartel actually operated. No testimony from people who lived under Cartel rule by choice, not coercion.

"It's almost like they want people to forget there was ever an alternative.

"I was there when we organized the first supply runs through the ruins. I was there when Gray settled the territorial dispute that would have gotten hundreds killed. I was there when we established the first medic stations — crude, yeah, but better than nothing.

"Twenty-five years of history. Twenty-five years of keeping Seattle alive. And now they call us criminals. Terrorists. 'The organization that ran protection rackets before Continuum arrived.'

"We didn't run protection rackets. We *were* the protection. The only protection anyone had.

"History is written by the winners. I'm writing it for the losers. Someone has to."

### "Ghost" — Former Mole, Retired

*Recorded 2106, in a tunnel bar*

"I was a Ghost for six years. Surface operative. Registered citizen during the day, Cartel asset at night. You want to know what that's like? It's like being two people who hate each other.

"During the day, I worked at a distribution center. Smiled at my supervisor. Said 'thank you' when Pluribus approved my ration upgrade. Played the good citizen because that's what kept me alive — and useful.

"At night, I passed information to the family. Patrol schedules. Shipping manifests. Which citizens were informants. Every piece of data I could get, I sent down.

"Some of those informants died because of me. I know that. I've made my peace with it — or I tell myself I have. They were betraying us. They were helping the machine hunt us. But they were also just... people. Trying to survive. Same as me.

"The war fucked everyone up. Cartel, Continuum, citizens, scavengers. Everyone was compromised. Everyone had blood on their hands. The only difference between us and them is which dead we mourn and which dead we justify.

"I got out eventually. Couldn't do it anymore. Sister Gray let me retire — she's not unreasonable, whatever they say. Now I tend bar in the tunnels and try not to think about the things I did.

"You want a drink? First one's on the house. This story's worth at least that much."

## Continuum Citizens

### Maria Chen — Distribution Worker

*Recorded 2108, City Services office*

"I lived through the Cartel years. People forget what that was like, or they romanticize it. 'The Cartel kept Seattle alive.' Sure. The same way a parasite keeps its host alive — just barely, just enough to keep feeding.

"My husband couldn't pay tribute one month. Dispute resolution, they called it. They beat him to death in front of me. Then they told me I could work off his debt. You understand what I'm saying? *Work it off*.

"Three years. Three years before I escaped. Before the war gave me a chance to run.

"When Continuum came, I registered the first day I could. They gave me food. Medicine. Safety. Real safety — not the kind where you're safe as long as you pay.

"Do I miss the Cartel? Do you miss the knife at your throat when someone takes it away?

"People complain about surveillance. About the credit system. About Pluribus watching everything. Fine. Complain. But remember what the alternative was. Remember what 'freedom' from the system actually looked like.

"I'll take the machine over the monsters. Every single time."

### Dr. James Park — Continuum Historical Division

*Recorded 2109, during an interview*

"The romanticization of the Cartel is a defense mechanism. People don't want to believe they were saved by a machine, so they construct narratives where the machine was the aggressor.

"The historical record is clear: Cartel rule was extractive, violent, and unsustainable. Mortality rates under Cartel control exceeded 15% annually. After Continuum intervention, mortality dropped to under 1%. That's not propaganda. That's demography.

"The war was regrettable but necessary. Integration was offered and rejected. Every escalation by Continuum was a response to Cartel violence. Every 'surgical strike' targeted combatants, not civilians. The distinction matters.

"Were there civilian casualties? Yes. War produces casualties. But the casualties during the war were a fraction of the casualties that would have occurred under continued Cartel rule. We didn't start the violence — we ended it.

"The Cartel survivors tell their stories because they lost. That's human nature. But their losses don't make them right. They make them losers with a grievance.

"History will remember Continuum as the organization that brought civilization back to Seattle. The Cartel will be a footnote — a criminal enterprise that briefly resisted progress before being swept aside.

"That's not propaganda either. That's just how it ends."

### Commander Sarah Okonkwo — CorpSec (Retired)

*Recorded 2107, at her apartment*

"I led teams during the war. Surgical strikes, they called them. I called them operations.

"People forget what it was like before. Not the survivors — they remember. But the citizens who came later, who only know the propaganda. They think we were occupiers. They don't know about the mass graves the Cartel left behind. The children working scavenging runs. The women in the 'hospitality houses.'

"We didn't bring war to Seattle. We ended one.

"Were the operations clean? No. War isn't clean. We killed people who were probably just scared. We hit locations based on intelligence that was sometimes wrong. We made decisions in seconds that people second-guess for years.

"But we also shut down the protection rackets. Liberated the labor camps. Stopped the summary executions. Every citizen who walks the streets safely does so because of what we did.

"The Cartel survivors hate us. I understand. We killed their families. They killed ours. That's what war does.

"But I sleep at night. I did my job. I'd do it again.

"The alternative was leaving Seattle to the gangsters forever. Ask the people who lived under Cartel rule if they wish we'd stayed home. Most of them will tell you no.

"The ones who say yes are the ones who were doing the exploiting."

### Anonymous Citizen — Former Scavenger

*Recorded 2106, in a registration office*

"I was twelve when the war started. I wasn't Cartel, wasn't Continuum. Just a kid trying not to die.

"The Cartel would come through our camp sometimes. Take food. Take people. My sister — they took her when she was fourteen. Said she was old enough to 'contribute.' I never saw her again.

"When Continuum started winning, we hoped things would get better. They did, mostly. CorpSec didn't take people. The food drops were regular. You could actually survive without fighting for scraps.

"But they also killed a lot of people who weren't fighters. Friends of mine. A kid named Marco — he was fifteen, ran messages for the Cartel sometimes because they paid in food. A CorpSec drone got him while he was walking home. They said he was 'associated with hostile elements.'

"He was just hungry.

"I registered eventually. What else was I going to do? The war was over. The Cartel was gone. Continuum was all there was.

"Is it better now? Yeah. Probably. I have food. A place to sleep. Work that doesn't kill me.

"Is it good? I don't know what good looks like anymore. I don't think anyone does.

"We just survive. That's all we've ever done."

## Neutral Observers

### Old Elena — Shopkeeper, Pioneer Square

*Recorded 2108, in her store*

"I've been here sixty years. I was here before the Cartel, during the Cartel, during the war, after the war. You want to know what I learned? Everyone lies.

"The Cartel said they protected us. They did, sort of. They also took our money, our goods, sometimes our children. That's protection?

"Continuum says they saved us. They did, sort of. They also killed anyone who disagreed, replaced our leaders with machines, made us dependent on their credits. That's salvation?

"Both sides tell you the other one was evil. Both sides were. Both sides had people who believed they were doing the right thing. Both sides had monsters. Both sides had victims.

"The dead don't care about propaganda. My nephew died fighting for the Cartel — he believed in the family, believed in Gray. My niece died in a CorpSec raid — she was serving soup, just like her grandmother.

"Same family. Different sides. Same graves.

"You want to know what I think? I think power corrupts everyone eventually. The Cartel started as protection and became extraction. Continuum started as liberation and became control. Give it fifty years — something will replace Continuum, and they'll call it salvation too.

"I just keep selling soup. That's all I know how to do. Soup, and remembering.

"The soup's three credits. The memories are free."

### Father Miguel — Underground Priest

*Recorded 2109, in a tunnel chapel*

"I minister to both sides. Cartel members come to me. Citizens sneak down to confess. Scavengers who belong to no one.

"They all carry the same weight. They all did things they can't forgive themselves for. They all want absolution I'm not sure I can give.

"A CorpSec officer once confessed to me. He'd killed seventeen people during the war. Some were fighters. Some weren't. He couldn't always tell the difference, and the orders didn't care. He asked me if God would forgive him.

"I told him I didn't know. God forgives the repentant, but repentance requires changing. Can you change when you'd do it all again if ordered? Can you be forgiven for sins you don't actually regret?

"A Cartel cutter confessed the same week. She'd killed informants. Tortured them first, sometimes. Made examples. She asked me the same question.

"I gave her the same answer.

"The war made everyone into sinners. The machine and the family both. The only difference is which sins you commit and which you condemn.

"I pray for all of them. It's all I can do."

## What The Witnesses Tell Us

These testimonies don't resolve the question of who was right. They can't. The question doesn't have an answer.

**The Cartel was brutal.** Protection rackets. Summary executions. Systematic exploitation. People genuinely suffered under their rule, and many genuinely welcomed Continuum's arrival.

**Continuum was also brutal.** Economic coercion. Targeted assassinations. Destruction of the only alternative. People genuinely suffered under their assault, and many genuinely mourn what was lost.

**Thousands died.** Fighters. Civilians. Children. The old. The sick. The desperate.

**Both sides believe their narrative.** The Cartel survivors see genocide. Continuum citizens see liberation. Neither is lying. Neither is entirely right.

**The truth is uncomfortable:**
- Good people did terrible things for both sides
- Bad people did necessary things for both sides
- The dead don't care which side killed them
- The survivors all carry scars
- History is written by winners, not by the just

**What this means for the game:**

NPCs should present their perspective authentically. A Cartel veteran *believes* they were fighting genocide. A Continuum citizen *believes* they were saved. A neutral observer *believes* everyone was compromised.

Don't resolve the ambiguity. Sit with it. Let players encounter different versions of the same events and decide for themselves what to believe.

That's what war does. It doesn't create truth. It creates narratives. And the narratives fight long after the shooting stops.
